Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: first production sprite sheet for an original fairy-tale enforcer in a Chrono Trigger-style SNES RPG.
Primary request: exactly sixteen fully separated poses of the SAME Briar Knight, a thorn-bound armored swordsman. A 4-column by 4-row sheet on a genuinely transparent background. No text, numbers, grid or scenery.
Character: compact adult male knight in articulated slate-steel plate, dark narrow visor with two tiny warm ivory eye glints, a small two-leaf dark green crest on his helmet, short thorny vines around his shoulder plates and shins, deep berry-purple split tabard, small gold rose-shaped chest clasp and buckle, muted green short cape visible from behind. Closed full helmet, no skin, no beard, no horns, no huge shoulder spikes. Strong recognizable silhouette. His sword is a straight narrow silver longsword with a small gold crossguard and berry-colored grip.
Style: expressive, carefully clustered 16-bit pixel art like a premium Chrono Trigger enemy sprite. Natural articulated pose changes, readable armor planes and gauntlets, restrained details; crisp pixels and dark outline. Do not use painted gradients, flat vector icons, glossy 3D or a tiny featureless toy figure.
Palette: #202a2f outline; #3c4c50 steel shadow; #70858a steel; #adbec1 steel highlight; #e5eddb pale metal; #28493c dark leaves; #50734a moss green; #91ad63 leaf highlights; #522e3d cloth shadow; #a64e65 berry cloth; #c2994d muted gold; #f5dfa2 warm highlight.
Framing: full bodies with generous transparent gutters. Same camera and exact same body scale in all cells. Upright bodies occupy only the lower two-thirds of each cell, leaving plenty of overhead margin for later sword poses. Slightly elevated RPG battle camera. Compact shoulders and cape: the upright body is about 26–28 native pixels wide and 38 native pixels tall, intended to fit within the center of a 64×80 native sprite frame. All hands, feet and crest must be present. No ground shadows.
