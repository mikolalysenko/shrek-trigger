# Empty future and Pinocchio's choice

`content/empty-future-dialogue.js` adapts all 327 referenced strings in 25 locations: Bangor and Trann Domes, Arris and its basement, both racing entrances, Proto Dome and its gate, the factory, sewers and the separate food-store event at location 344. The 21 native decision boxes, race modes, score arguments, button passwords, rewards and event branches retain their original instructions. Only the B8 text-table operands change.

The future first resembles a closed attraction. Trann's rest machine restores the body without feeding it. Arris shelters people whose food store is guarded by machines. The failed cooling system and the seeds in a dead traveler's hand lead into the archive's record of the apocalypse. The party chooses to change that future; the hesitation answer remains available. Doan accepts the seeds and gives the race-bike key. The racing rival is Rivet, a remaining attraction performer, and all prizes use the matching onion names. The archive has no comic narration of the catastrophe.

Pinocchio is a discarded puppet, not a machine that must become human. Donkey repairs his joints and strings. He remembers his own name; the native naming screen supplies a short entry for the travel book rather than imposing a replacement identity. Either Fiona or Donkey can remain at the workshop door. His brothers condemn independent action, attack him, and fight the remaining two party members. Both companion branches carry him out. The second repair ends with his decision to help and Fiona's acceptance of him. The real gate then carries all four companions to the Last Inn.

## Sprite and cartridge budget

The six brothers use enemy 248, whose native header already shares player 3's uncompressed graphics, assembly and animation bank. They therefore inherit all 256 original Pinocchio poses from `engine/pinocchio-art.js`, including the source blank frames. `content/workshop-puppets.js` supplies a private twelve-color palette with warm wood and a burgundy uniform. Palette 215 has no other header owner or pointer alias. Their size, combat header, statistics and AI remain unchanged. The palette and battle name require no new graphics, assembly, WRAM or VRAM allocation.

The current build has 1,837 adapted entries in 100 edited events. With the subsequent machine and race art it remains 4 MiB, with 52,942 bytes free in audited pools. The 292 reclaimed original dialogue spans total 57,354 bytes. These are build measurements, not a guarantee that later assets will fit without further packing work.

## Reversible checkpoints

Twenty-five presets cover arrival, the Lab 16 scavengers, the northern sewer entrance and lower sound traps, the supply keeper, food store, rat chase, both passage switches, archive, seed return, Rivet's introduction and both race modes, the ruined road and recorder chest, first repair, factory security, crane, conveyor beetles, lab defenses, final door lock, generator, each brother-fight companion and second repair. Each fixes its own party and progression flags. The first-repair preset marks the two earlier workshop entrance fights complete; it does not bypass the repair, naming screen or companion choice. The door-lock and generator presets include the earlier lab-laser shutdown so the escape ladder is reachable. The brother presets begin after the power-core escape. The second-repair preset begins after the party has carried Pinocchio back. All use the strong developer loadout and restore the previous machine state with Return from jump. They are absent from the IPS patch.

The recruitment audit corrected the developer tools' character bitmask. The native table at file `3FF9BB` assigns bits `$80,$40,$20,$10,$08,$04,$02` to the seven characters. `$7E29AF` is the joined-character mask; bit zero is unused. The native roster spans nine bytes at `$7E2980`, including inactive entries. Generic party shortcuts and all chapter fixtures now use that layout. This correction changes diagnostics only, not the game's native recruitment routines.

`tests/empty-future.test.mjs` verifies byte-for-byte event preservation, choices, controls, arrival, the seed discovery, both archive answers and recording transitions, the seed return and actual key ID, both recruitment parties, the native naming screen, both six-puppet battles and recoveries, second repair and arrival at the Last Inn. `tests/workshop-puppets.test.mjs` compares every frame's native VRAM upload and checks palette ownership and unchanged AI. Browser checks cover all twenty-four presets, fixed parties, undo and the puppet preview. The independent runner repeats the scenes with the same cartridge in Snes9x and bsnes and checks puppet tiles and displayed pixels.

`tests/future-puzzles.test.mjs` drives the rat from the first warning to a real B-dash/A-catch. It checks that the password is learned, that the two passage switches reject missing shoulder buttons and an unknown password, and that each switch opens and closes. Factory tests exercise Pinocchio's security tag, wrong crane inputs, both XA/BB barrel moves, repeating a used crane code, a wrong final password, X A B Y, reopening the lock, the lab-laser shutdown and ambush, the generator emergency, Pinocchio holding the doors and the actual ladder transition back to the lab. The test reads coordinates to steer ordinary controller input; it does not change movement speed or puzzle scripts. Every fixture verifies unchanged cartridge bytes and exact WRAM restoration on undo. These scene routes do not yet constitute a continuous normal-level factory traversal.
